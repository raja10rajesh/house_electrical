Solar, battery backup and private metering for six floors

Two net meters from TGSPDCL, the power company, feed the building. Meter 2 carries the PuREPower hybrid inverter, 14 kW of solar panels, the heavier floors and the common pumps. Meter 1 is a net meter too; it has no generation behind it yet. The car charger, when fitted, sits after Meter 2 and before the inverter. Lights, fans and the lift never blink in a power cut. The backup bus is planned to 17.5 kW, not the 20 kVA nameplate.

What each floor pays (monthly average)

Ground (common) units are billed at the same rate and shared equally by the five homes. A vacant home pays nothing; its use and its common share fall on the owner.

Why the meter split matters: the tariff slab resets every month

TGSPDCL's home tariff is progressive and each connection's slab starts again from zero every month, so the average rate climbs fast as a connection's monthly units grow - whether or not solar sits behind it. The dot shows where Meter 1 sits today at the chosen layout; keeping it left of the 800-unit line (dashed) avoids the higher fixed charge too.

Contingency: if TGSPDCL grants only one meter

Worth planning for. If only one connection is sanctioned for the plot, the inverter and every floor's heavy loads end up behind the same meter: there is no second connection left to keep a clean, solar-backed slab. The wiring, backup design and billing method stay the same; only the meter split disappears. Flip “Viewing” at the top of the page to see this reflected in every diagram, flow simulation and calculation on this site.
